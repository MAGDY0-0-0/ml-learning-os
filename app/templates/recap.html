The ML math recap sheet

You've done Linear Algebra and Calc 1–2. These are the ~15 headlines that actually recur in M4–M6. Read, tick what's solid, and only patch what isn't.

How to use this: anything you read and think "yes, obviously" — done, never revisit. Anything that makes you pause, note it; the next unit links a single 8–15 minute chapter for each. The expected outcome for someone fresh out of Calc 2 is that you tick almost everything and skip the patch unit entirely.

Linear algebra

1. A matrix is a linear map. Multiplying by W takes a vector somewhere else, linearly. Neural network layers are exactly this, plus a bias and a nonlinearity.

2. Shapes. (a,b) @ (b,c) = (a,c). Inner dimensions must match and they vanish. Most deep-learning bugs you'll hit are this rule violated.

3. Dot product = similarity. a·b = |a||b|cos θ. Normalise both and you get cosine similarity — which is the entire basis of embedding search in M7.

4. Transpose swaps rows and columns; it appears constantly in backprop because the gradient of xW w.r.t. x involves Wᵀ.

5. Eigen-intuition (one line). An eigenvector is a direction the matrix only stretches, never rotates. You need this for PCA in M4 and essentially nowhere else early on.

6. Rank / linear independence. Redundant features = linearly dependent columns = an ill-conditioned problem. This is why collinearity hurts linear models.

Calculus

7. A derivative is a local slope. Gradient descent walks downhill using it. Nothing more mystical is going on.

8. The gradient is the vector of partial derivatives — it points in the direction of steepest increase, so we step against it.

9. The chain rule, in the exact form backprop uses: dL/dx = dL/dy · dy/dx. Backpropagation is this applied repeatedly, right to left, through the computation graph. If you understand this line you understand M5's first unit.

10. Why we need convexity language. Neural network losses are non-convex, so "the minimum" isn't guaranteed — we settle for a good local one. That's why seeds and initialisation matter.

Probability & statistics

11. Probability vs likelihood. Probability: parameters fixed, data varies. Likelihood: data fixed, parameters vary. Maximum Likelihood Estimation maximises the second — and cross-entropy loss is negative log-likelihood.

12. Expectation and variance. Mean and spread. You will report both for every ablation in M5; a mean without a spread supports no claim.

13. Bayes' rule. P(A|B) = P(B|A)P(A)/P(B). Underpins naive Bayes, and the intuition for why base rates matter when your classes are imbalanced.

14. The Gaussian. Why it's everywhere: the central limit theorem, plus it's mathematically convenient. Weight initialisation samples from it.

15. Bias–variance. High bias = train and validation error both high and close (underfitting). High variance = low train error, much higher validation error (overfitting). You will read this off learning curves constantly from M4 onward.

What you do NOT need right now

Measure theory, proofs of convergence, matrix calculus identities beyond the chain rule, information geometry, most of functional analysis. If a resource starts there, it's the wrong resource for this stage — swap it.
